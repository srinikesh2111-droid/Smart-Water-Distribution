LIKELY VIVA QUESTIONS
Q: Why BFS? A: It checks reachability in an unweighted graph using a queue and visited set; adjacency-list BFS is O(V+E), apart from the current implementation's repeated edge lookup.
Q: Why an adjacency list? A: The network is sparse and the list stores only existing links.
Q: How is over-allocation prevented? A: Reservoir.allocateWater caps delivery at remaining supply, and WaterRequest.recordAllocation caps cumulative delivery at the request's unmet amount.
Q: How are priorities implemented? A: DemandArea subclasses override allocationPriority(); the policy orders critical, high, normal and applies deterministic ID tie-breaking.
Q: What happens when a pipe fails? A: The edge remains in the topology but BFS only traverses it if its Pipeline is working, so it is excluded from reachable paths.
Q: Is this a hydraulic simulator? A: No. It models connectivity and simplified allocation, not pressure, leakage or pipe capacity.
Q: Does data persist after restart? A: Not currently; the state is in memory. Database integration is pending confirmation of permitted technology.
Q: What tests were run? A: Compilation and 38 dependency-free model checks on OpenJDK 21. Full GUI click automation was not performed; we manually verify the visible interactions on the demo machine.

DO NOT CLAIM: database integration, persistent storage, measured performance, real-world water savings, or fully automated GUI tests unless these are subsequently implemented and verified.
